**Researchers handed IBS patients a bottle clearly labelled "placebo pills" and told them the capsules held nothing active. After three weeks, 59% of them reported adequate relief from their symptoms. In a matched group that got no pills, 35% did.**

The trial, led by Ted Kaptchuk at Beth Israel Deaconess Medical Center and Harvard Medical School, was published in PLOS ONE in 2010. It tested a simple question: does a placebo still work if nobody is fooled?

---

## How the trial worked

- **80 adults** with irritable bowel syndrome (IBS) took part, from August 2009 to April 2010, at one academic medical centre in Boston.
- 37 were given open-label placebo pills: "placebo pills made of an inert substance, like sugar pills", in a bottle that said so, with the instruction to take **2 pills twice a day**.
- The other 43 got no pills.
- Both groups had the same kind of visits, with the same amount of time and attention from the same clinicians.

## What they found

- **59% vs 35%:** the share reporting adequate relief at the 21-day endpoint, placebo group vs no-pill group.
- **92 points:** how much the placebo group's symptom severity score improved from the start. The paper says a 50-point improvement "reliably indicates meaningful symptomatic improvement."
- The placebo group also scored higher on overall improvement at both the 11-day and 21-day checks.

The authors note that typical placebo responses in hidden, double-blind IBS drug trials run at **30 to 40%**, so the 59% here was higher than usual. They suggest that knowing for certain you are getting the treatment being tested may itself help.

## What it does not show

This was one small trial at one centre, over three weeks. Patients were told, in a hopeful way, that placebos have helped people with IBS through "mind-body self-healing processes", so the explanation may be part of the effect. The authors call it a "proof-of-principle" pilot study and say it needs repeating with more people and a longer follow-up. It is not a reason to stop any treatment. Talk to a doctor about IBS.
