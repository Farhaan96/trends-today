**Your phone's blue dot only works because GPS corrects for Einstein.** Atomic clocks on the satellites run **38 microseconds per day faster** than clocks on Earth, the US National Institute of Standards and Technology says. Skip that correction and, NASA says, position errors pile up so fast the system would be **useless for navigation in a matter of minutes**.

---

## Where the 38 comes from

Two relativity effects fight each other:

- **Special relativity:** the satellites move so fast that their clocks should fall **about 7 microseconds behind** earthbound clocks each day.
- **General relativity:** weaker gravity in medium Earth orbit should speed them up by **about 45 microseconds per day**.

Combine them and the clocks run **38 microseconds per day fast**. NIST and NASA both give those same three numbers.

## Why minutes matter

GPS times radio signals from satellites about **20,000 km** up. Timing has to be precise to billionths of a second. NASA says the relativity effects are **nearly 1,000 times** larger than the timing precision GPS needs. Without the Einstein correction, errors grow until navigation fails in minutes.

Engineers built the correction in from the start so satellite clocks stay in step with clocks on Earth.
