**Wait, what?** The United States now spends more paying interest on its debt than it spends on its military. Not a forecast. It already happened, and it happened again this year.

In fiscal 2026, which ran from October 2025 to September 2026, the US paid **$1,051.8 billion in net interest**. National defense got **$965.2 billion**. Those are the full year totals in Table 9 of the [US Treasury's Monthly Treasury Statement](https://fiscaldata.treasury.gov/datasets/monthly-treasury-statement/).

That is a gap of **$86.6 billion** in favour of interest (our math). Spread over 365 days, the interest bill works out to about **$2.9 billion a day** (our math again).

## Musk said it out loud

Elon Musk brought it up in [his interview with Walter Isaacson on The Builders](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AQnwQHMnCrE&t=934s), titled "Elon Musk: The End of Work, the Death of Money, and a World Run by AI."

At 15:34 he said: "you've got an interest on the debt that exceeds what we spent on the military. Um, which is a really an astonishing amount of interest to be paid."

On the Treasury's numbers, he is right.

## This is not a one year blip

Interest has been climbing fast. Here is how the two lines compare, using Treasury figures:

| Fiscal year | Net interest | National defense | Which is bigger |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2024 | $881.7 billion | $874.0 billion | Interest, barely |
| 2025 | $970.4 billion | $916.6 billion | Interest |
| 2026 | $1,051.8 billion | $965.2 billion | Interest |

Fiscal 2024 was the crossover year, by just $7.7 billion (our math). By 2026 the gap was more than ten times that.

The fiscal 2025 figures also appear in the [Treasury's Combined Statement for fiscal 2025](https://fiscal.treasury.gov/system/files/files/reports-statements/combined-statement/cs2025/outlay.pdf), which says net interest outlays were $970.4 billion and national defense outlays were $916.6 billion.

## His fix: AI and robots

Musk did not stop at the diagnosis. At 14:59 he said: "I came to the conclusion that the only way we're going to solve the national debt is with AI and robots."

Then, at 15:08: "We're not going to solve it by trying to save our way there. The appetite is not there."

That part is **his opinion**, not a fact anyone can check today. The interest bill is real. Whether robots can pay it down is a bet on the future.

## The fine print

**Which "interest"?** We use net interest, the Treasury's budget function 900. That is the standard measure. Gross interest on Treasury securities is bigger still, because it includes interest paid to federal trust funds, but it is not the usual yardstick.

**Which "military"?** We use national defense, budget function 050. It covers the Department of Defense plus things like nuclear weapons work at the Department of Energy. The claim also holds for the Pentagon's military programs on their own: $916.8 billion in fiscal 2026, per Monthly Treasury Statement Table 5.

**Where it flips.** If you count veterans benefits and services as military spending too, that adds $416.2 billion in fiscal 2026. Defense plus veterans comes to $1,381.4 billion (our math), which is more than net interest. So the headline holds for defense spending, not for every possible definition of "military."

**One restatement.** Treasury later revised fiscal 2024 net interest to $881.0 billion. That is still above defense, so 2024 stays the crossover year.

**Our math.** The gap, the per day figure and the defense plus veterans total are our own calculations from the Treasury numbers above.
