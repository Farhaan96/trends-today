**Most people who buy new Solana memecoins lose money.** Bitquery followed about 2.9 million ordinary wallets that bought new coins on Solana launchpads such as Pump.fun, in one launch week a month from November 2025 to August 2026. Two weeks later, **74% had less SOL than they put in, after fees.**

You would not guess that from your feed. A post went viral claiming a five-agent AI trading bot turned $3,000 into $12,500 overnight by sniping new Solana coins. We are not naming the account, and we cannot check the claim. What we can check is what happens to everyone else.

_This is education, not financial advice._

## Finding 1: the typical buyer loses a little

Bitquery counted a wallet as an ordinary buyer if it paid its own fees, made fewer than 100 swaps on its busiest day and was not a known bot or the coin's creator. Across those wallets:

- **74%** ended with less SOL two weeks after buying, after fees.
- **The median buyer lost $13.** Half lost more, half lost less or came out ahead.
- **Together they lost about $269 million** in the 10 launch weeks Bitquery followed.

A second, independent dataset points the same way. A public Dune dashboard by the analyst web3frank looked at about 7 million wallets that traded Pump.fun tokens from November 2025 to May 2026. Its summary puts the **median realized profit at $0.02**. Two cents, for six months of trading.

## Finding 2: the gains go to very few

Some buyers do win, and that is the point. Bitquery found **the best 1% of ordinary buyers took 56% of all the money won.** Most of the rest went to the next 9%. Nine in ten buyers shared what was left, and most of them lost.

The steadiest winners were not people at all. **Sandwich bots**, which trade just before and just after a buyer to take a slice of each trade, came out ahead in every launch week Bitquery checked.

## Finding 3: launch insiders beat the sniper bots

A separate Bitquery investigation looked at who buys a Pump.fun coin in its first moments, for launches from October 30, 2025 to June 30, 2026.

- **Sniper bots**: 25,882 wallets that buy new coins within a fraction of a second. They spent **$440 million**, and made **$31.5 million** on what they sold. Counting the coins they could not sell as worthless, they made **$15.2 million**, and **only 54% of sniper wallets came out ahead.**
- **Launch bundles**: wallets that a coin's creator packs into the launch itself, so they buy before anyone can see the coin. Creators and their bundled wallets made **at least $579 million**, **18 times or more** what the sniper bots made.

So even fast, automated sniping is close to a coin flip for the wallets that try it. The surest money sits with whoever controls the launch.

## Why "my bot made 4x overnight" posts mislead

This is survivorship bias. You see the wallet that won, not the thousands that lost on the same coins the same night.

- **The winners are real, and rare.** When the best 1% take 56% of the gains, there will always be a screenshot of someone doing very well.
- **The losers do not post.** A loss of $13 makes no headline. Spread over 2.9 million wallets, it added up to about $269 million.
- **One night is not a strategy.** A single good run says little about what the same bot does over months, after fees and after the coins it could not sell.
- **Bots are not a shortcut.** Bitquery's sniper bots are exactly the kind of fast, automated buyers these posts describe, and close to half of them did not come out ahead.

## The limits

- Bitquery and Dune count wallets, not people. One person can run many wallets.
- Bitquery followed each buyer for two weeks and counted unsold coins as worth nothing. It left out tips and trading-app fees, so it says real losses are a little larger.
- The Dune figures come from one analyst's dashboard and count realized profit only, for wallets that both bought and sold. Its live query tables, which can update, now show a median of a few cents rather than exactly two.
- The two Bitquery studies cover different windows, and none of these figures predicts future results.
