**Someone collected a fortune in gold from an Air Canada cargo bay at Toronto Pearson with a fake waybill. Years later, a Federal Court capped the airline's payout to Brinks at 9,988 special drawing rights, reported as about $18,500.**

The gold left Zurich on Air Canada flight AC881 and arrived April 17, 2023. Shortly after landing, the shipments were stolen from Air Canada Cargo at Pearson. The court record says suspects presented a fraudulent air waybill.

---

## What went missing

From Justice Strickland's reasons in **2025 FC 110**:

- **Gold:** 400.19 kg of gold bars, booked value **13,612,696.75 CHF**.
- **Banknotes:** 53.18 kg, value **$1,945,843 USD**.
- Brinks reimbursed the clients: **$14,988,920.58 USD** to Valcambi and **1,734,835 CHF** to Raiffeisen.

CP24's report on the judgment described about **6,600 gold bars** worth **$20 million**, plus about **$2.5 million** in foreign currency.

## Why the airline owed so little in court

Canada applies the Montreal Convention to international air cargo. Without a valid special declaration of interest and any required supplementary sum, carrier liability is capped by weight: **22 SDR per kilogram**.

The court found Brinks had not made that special declaration for these shipments. Air Canada's cross-motion won. Liability: **8,811 SDR** for the gold and **1,177 SDR** for the banknotes (**9,988 SDR** total). CP24 called that about **$18,500**.

That is the civil liability fight between Brinks and Air Canada. It is separate from the criminal investigation and charges reported around the heist.
