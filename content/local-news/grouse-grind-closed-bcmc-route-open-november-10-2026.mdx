**The Grouse Grind Trail is closed from October 13 to November 10** for annual maintenance and trail upgrades, and Metro Vancouver says the **closure is in effect seven days a week** while work is underway. There is no weekend opening.

Metro Vancouver's [park alerts page](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park-alerts-and-notices) says the **BCMC Route remains open** and offers a similar challenge, ending at the same destination as the Grouse Grind Trail. The [official Grouse Mountain Regional Park map](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/grouse-mountain-regional-park-map.pdf) shows both routes climbing from the park entrance toward the Grouse Mountain Resort.

The detail most people will miss is the clock: the park closes at **4 p.m.** until November 10, and Metro Vancouver lists the BCMC climb at 2 hours or more plus about 2 hours to walk back down it, so anyone hiking both ways should start well before noon.

---

## What to know

**Closed:** Grouse Grind Trail, Grouse Mountain Regional Park, North Vancouver.

**When:** October 13 to November 10, seven days a week.

**Why:** Annual maintenance and trail upgrades.

**Still open:** The BCMC Route. Metro Vancouver lists it at 2.9 km with 750 m of elevation gain, 2 hours or more of hiking time, rated challenging. The park brochure says it is more rugged and on rougher terrain than a trail, which typically has more built features such as steps, bridges and handrails.

**Park hours:** October 16 to November 10, 7 a.m. to 4 p.m.

**Dogs:** Not allowed on the Grouse Grind Trail or the BCMC Route. The Baden-Powell Trail is leash-required.

**Parking:** Metro Vancouver lists pay parking at Lot D, $2 for 3 hours or $4 for all day. The park is also accessible by transit.

---

## What to do

- Do not plan a Grind climb before November 10. Check the [Grouse Grind Trail page](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/grouse-grind) before you go, since conditions can change.
- If you take the BCMC Route, Metro Vancouver says to wear hiking shoes with good ankle support, wear warm layers, and bring plenty of water and snacks.
- Plan your way down before you start. Metro Vancouver's Grouse Grind page lists two ways back: the Grouse Mountain Resort gondola, which charges a fee, or the BCMC Route, 2.9 km and about 2 hours to descend. If you are counting on the gondola, confirm with the resort that it is running.
- Bring a headlamp or flashlight. Metro Vancouver notes that darkness comes quickly this time of year, especially in the forest.
- Expect weather closures. The park brochure says trails may close during high winds, heavy rain, heavy snow or icy conditions.
- If you meet a bear or cougar, the brochure says to keep 100 m away, talk in a calm voice, slowly back away and leave the area. Do not run.
- To report trail conditions, call the Grouse Mountain area office at 604-224-5739. In an emergency, call 9-1-1.
