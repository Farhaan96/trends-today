**Can you do a Lighthouse Park day trip from Vancouver by bus?** Yes. Take TransLink bus **#250** toward Horseshoe Bay, get off at westbound **Marine Drive at Beacon Lane**, carefully cross Marine Drive, and walk Beacon Lane into the park (about a six-minute walk from the stop). The District of West Vancouver lists park hours **7 a.m. to 10 p.m.** daily. Walk the trail network to the **Point Atkinson lighthouse viewpoint** (the light station grounds themselves are closed to the public). Budget about **25 minutes** uphill from the viewpoint back to the parking lot. Prefer the bus on sunny weekends. If you drive, expect year-round pay parking at **$5.08 per hour** (including taxes) from **7 a.m. to 10 p.m.** Confirm Trip Planner times and trail notices before you leave. Do not rely on the **#257 Express** for this stop.

## Why Lighthouse Park works as a half day

Lighthouse Park covers about **75 hectares** at the south end of Beacon Lane in West Vancouver. District materials call it a nationally significant natural park with remnant old-growth coastal forest and high visitor use. The lighthouse and light station at Point Atkinson form a National Historic Site and one of the Lower Mainland's most recognizable landmarks.

You do not need a paid attraction ticket to walk here. Once you are on site you have a dense trail network, ocean headland views, and a clear viewpoint for the lighthouse. That is the point of this planner: land by bus, walk a forest loop to the viewpoint, and get home without gambling on a scarce stall.

Set expectations. This is a wilderness-style District park with cliffs, steep terrain, and dense forest. Some trails are challenging. Wear real walking shoes or hiking boots. The light station grounds themselves cannot be accessed by the public. Use the park viewpoint the District points out on its trail map.

Sunny weekends crowd Beacon Lane and fill the parking lot fast. Pay parking applies year-round if you drive. Prefer the #250 so the half day is not a parking hunt.

If you liked the forest loops of [Lynn Canyon](/things-to-do/lynn-canyon-day-trip-guide-2026) or the North Shore village feel of [Deep Cove](/things-to-do/deep-cove-day-trip-guide-2026), Lighthouse Park is the West Vancouver headland sibling that stays on the #250 corridor.

## How to get there without a car

### Bus #250 (preferred)

Take TransLink bus **#250** (Horseshoe Bay / Dundarave / Vancouver) westbound along Marine Drive. The nearest stop for the park is **Marine Drive at Beacon Lane**. Transit apps commonly put the walk from that stop at about **six minutes**. Carefully cross busy Marine Drive, then follow Beacon Lane toward the park entrance and parking area.

Treat Trip Planner times as typical. Marine Drive traffic and North Shore weather can slow the run. Build a buffer if you are aiming for an early trail start.

Ask the driver to call the Beacon Lane stop if you are new to the corridor. Outbound buses toward Horseshoe Bay pass local landmarks before the stop; Trip Planner remains the source of truth the morning you go.

### Do not use the #257 Express for this park

The **#257 Horseshoe Bay / Vancouver Express** is faster for Horseshoe Bay itself, but it is the wrong tool for Lighthouse Park. Express service skips the local Marine Drive stops nearest Beacon Lane. Stick to the **#250** (or another local Marine Drive route Trip Planner suggests for that stop) when the park is the goal.

No buses are permitted down Water Lane or into the Lighthouse Park parking lot. The last stretch is always on foot from Marine Drive / Beacon Lane.

### Confirm before you leave

Use [TransLink Trip Planner](https://www.translink.ca/) or the Transit app the morning you go. Door-to-park time from downtown Vancouver is often cited around **40 minutes** on a good day in older car-free guides. That is a rough claim, not a door-to-door promise from your couch.

## Driving and pay parking (if you skip transit)

Prefer the bus so the half day is not a parking hunt. District materials are blunt: public parking at the park is limited and fills up quickly in good weather. Have an alternate plan.

If you do drive, turn onto **Beacon Lane** from Marine Drive when travelling toward Horseshoe Bay, then follow signs for Lighthouse Park.

Pay parking is in effect **year-round** for Lighthouse Park between **7 a.m. and 10 p.m.**, seven days a week. The District Destination Parks Pay Parking FAQ lists the hourly rate for Lighthouse Park (and sibling destination parks such as Whytecliff and Whyte Lake / Nelson Canyon) at **$5.08 per hour**, including applicable taxes. There is no day-pass option on that FAQ.

Payment options listed by the District:

- hangTag app or QR-linked hangTag website
- PayByPhone app or call-in
- Impark website for West Vancouver parks
- Physical pay station at Lighthouse Park (credit card **insert**; contactless tap is not available)

A small convenience fee can apply once per mobile transaction (District FAQ cites hangTag **$0.30** and PayByPhone **$0.35**). Download an app and set up an account before you arrive if you can. Signage in the lot shows the location number.

Exemptions listed by the District include vehicles with Veteran or Memorial Cross license plates and vehicles with a valid accessible parking placard. District of West Vancouver residents may apply for an annual parking pass at no cost through [impark.com/westvan](https://impark.com/westvan) after address verification.

## Hour-by-hour half-day outline

Use this as a template, not a stopwatch.

1. **Late morning #250.** Board toward Horseshoe Bay with slack. Do not cut a tight transfer if you want a calm trail start.
2. **Walk Beacon Lane into the park.** Orient at the information board. Grab the District self-guiding trail materials if available and open the [trail map](https://www.westvancouver.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails/lighthouse-park-trails) on your phone.
3. **Choose a loop toward the lighthouse viewpoint.** Trails are relatively short but interconnected. Some are steep. Stay on marked paths.
4. **Viewpoint buffer.** Photograph the lighthouse from the public viewpoint. Do not expect access to the light station grounds.
5. **Uphill return.** District trail materials ask you to plan for about a **25-minute** hike uphill from the lighthouse area back to the parking lot. That is the energy cost many visitors underestimate.
6. **Washrooms and exit.** Portable toilets sit near the parking lot entrances (Birdsong Path side and Beacon Lane side). Walk back to Marine Drive for the eastbound #250 toward Vancouver. Leave buffer before evening plans.

## Trails and the lighthouse viewpoint

Explore the network of trails through remnant old-growth forest down toward the historic lighthouse on the water. District trail materials rate overall difficulty as **moderate**, with distance and elevation variable by route. Refer to the official trail map rather than inventing kilometre totals.

**Birdsong Path** is the short accessible option. It begins at the parking lot entrance and follows a gentle route through wetland and forest to an open area. Use it if you want a lower-stress first loop or if mobility needs matter.

Remember the District's wilderness warning: cliffs, steep terrain, and dense forest. Wet roots after rain make footing worse. Fog can hide cliff edges. Stay behind any barriers and keep children close near viewpoints.

A functioning lighthouse has remained at this National Historic Site since the 1870s. The waters around the park are noted as a Rockfish Protection Area. Birders come for diving cormorants and soaring eagles. Picnic spots exist, but treat this as a leave-no-trace forest day, not a BBQ destination (see rules below).

## Dogs, rules, and washrooms

**Dogs:** Well-behaved dogs that stay on the trails are allowed **off-leash** on the trails in Lighthouse Park, **except Birdsong Path**. On Birdsong Path, dogs must be on a leash no greater than **two metres** and under control, with a maximum of **two dogs per person**. Check the District [Dogs in Parks](https://www.westvancouver.ca/parks-recreation/parks-trails/dogs-parks) page for licensing and related rules.

**Prohibited year-round in West Vancouver parks and beaches (District fire and parks notices):**

- Open fires, including beach fires
- Smoking or vaping in parks and beaches
- Charcoal and briquette barbecues in parks and beaches
- **All barbecues in Lighthouse Park**

**Also not allowed without special permission:** recreational drones in West Vancouver parks, and commercial activities or vending in parks.

**Washrooms:** Two portable toilets near the Birdsong Path parking entrance (one accessible) and two portable toilets near the Beacon Lane parking entrance.

## Weather, crowds, and trail honesty

Sunny weekends fill the lot and the popular viewpoint loops. If you want quieter photos, go earlier on a shoulder day and arrive by bus.

Rain makes roots and rock slick. Good shoes beat fashion sneakers. Ocean wind at the headland can feel colder than the downtown forecast, so pack a layer even on mild September days.

Fall is a strong season for forest colour and cooler hiking, but it is not a reason to ignore cliff edges or fade-to-dark timing. The park closes at **10 p.m.** Plan to be back at Marine Drive with bus buffer before you are tired and cold.
