**Is Deer Lake Park worth a fall day trip?** Yes, if you want an easy lake walk with a free art gallery and heritage gardens attached. The City of Burnaby says Deer Lake Park is open **dawn to dusk year-round**, with trails circling the lake past marsh, forest, and meadow. In October 2026 plan around three things. The **boardwalk by the boat launch is closed** for a full replacement (signed detour, about five extra minutes). The **Shadbolt Centre north surface lot is fully closed from Sept. 28 to Oct. 21**. And **Burnaby Village Museum is between seasons** until Haunted Village on Oct. 23. By transit, the area is served by the **144 SFU Exchange/Metrotown Station** bus. Use TransLink Trip Planner for the stop and time from where you start. Lots close at **10 p.m.** every night.

## Why Deer Lake works as a fall half day

Deer Lake sits in the middle of Burnaby but feels farther out once you are on the trail. The City calls it the place "where nature, culture and heritage meet," and that is a fair summary. Walking trails connect the lake to the **Burnaby Art Gallery**, the **Shadbolt Centre for the Arts**, **Burnaby Village Museum**, and **Hart House** restaurant.

The wildlife is the quiet draw. The City says to watch for great blue herons fishing in the shallows, turtles sunning on logs, red-tailed hawks overhead, and sometimes beavers working in the marshy west end of the lake. In fall, the mix of marsh, forest, and the planted gardens near the lake is what makes it a good colour walk. Colour timing changes every year with the weather, so treat any peak date you read online as a guess.

The [free fall colour walks near Vancouver](/things-to-do/free-fall-colour-walks-near-vancouver-2026) roundup lists Deer Lake. This is the full single-park plan.

## How to get there (transit first)

Burnaby's Deer Lake Park page sends transit riders to **TransLink Trip Planner** rather than naming a stop, so this guide does the same for the exact stop. What the City page does show is that the **144 SFU Exchange/Metrotown Station** bus serves the Deer Lake and City Hall area. Its September concert notice described the 144 detouring to the City Hall bus loop and Canada Way, both on the edge of the park. The 144 connects **Metrotown Station** on the Expo Line with **SFU Exchange** on Burnaby Mountain.

A simple plan: Expo Line to Metrotown Station, then the 144 toward SFU, getting off near City Hall or Deer Lake Avenue as Trip Planner shows. This guide does not invent headways or walk times. Check Trip Planner the morning you go.

## Driving and parking honesty

The City of Burnaby lists three parking areas for Deer Lake Park and asks you to park only in designated areas. **Parking is not permitted in the residential areas around the park.**

- **Deer Lake Avenue lots.** The Shadbolt Centre, Burnaby Art Gallery, and Burnaby Village Museum lots are reached from Deer Lake Avenue. The Village Museum page says parking in its lot is free but limited.
- **Sperling Avenue lot.** This is the one closest to the beach, dock, and playground at the east end of the lake. It has **7 designated accessible stalls**, and there is an accessible washroom nearby.
- **Royal Oak Avenue lot.** On the west side of the park, handy if you want to start at the marshy end.

All park lots close at **10 p.m.** every night.

The October catch is the Shadbolt Centre. Its page (checked Oct. 8, 2026) says the **north surface lot on Deer Lake Avenue is fully closed from Sept. 28 to Oct. 21** for new asphalt and entranceway construction, and it points visitors to the **underground lot**. If you planned to park there for the gallery or the Festival Lawn, either use the underground lot, try Sperling, or take the bus.

This guide does not list parking rates because the City pages checked today do not post any for these lots. If you see a meter or a sign, trust the sign.

## Hour-by-hour half-day outline

1. **Late morning arrival.** Bus in on the 144 or park at Sperling. Starting at the Sperling end puts the washroom, beach, and playground right at the start.
2. **Walk the lake.** The City lists four routes: the **Deer Lake Loop Route**, the **Hart House Mini Route**, **Oakland Flats**, and the **Royal Oak Meadow Route**. Download the City trails map before you go, since the boat launch boardwalk is closed and the signed detour runs on Sperling Avenue and Deer Lake Drive.
3. **Gardens stop.** On the southeast shore, the **Eagles Estate** heritage garden has Japanese maples, dwarf Alberta spruce, rhododendrons, and magnolias, designed by horticulturist Frank E. Buck. Near the gallery, **Century Gardens** is the City's showcase for rhododendrons, Burnaby's official flower. You can also see the outside of the **Deer Lake heritage homes**, built between 1904 and 1935, and Arthur Erickson's **Baldwin House** on the south shore.
4. **Free gallery hour.** Walk up to the **Burnaby Art Gallery** in Ceperley House. Admission is always free.
5. **Picnic or head home.** Picnic tables sit near the wide paved path below the gallery. Leave with daylight to spare, since the park closes at dusk.

## The cultural sites and what is open in October

### Burnaby Art Gallery

The City says it is the only public art museum in Canada dedicated to collecting works on paper, and it holds more than 6,000 works. Exhibitions change regularly and **admission is always free**, with a **suggested $10 donation**. Hours posted today: **Tuesday to Friday 10 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.** and **Saturday and Sunday noon to 5 p.m.** Mondays are not listed. Address: 6344 Deer Lake Avenue.

### Shadbolt Centre for the Arts

The Shadbolt hosts performances, arts programs, and festivals, and its main building stays open during construction of a new theatre and art studios. Its **Encores Cafe is closed** and the City says it will reopen in 2027 when the new building is done. Do not count on a coffee stop here.

### Burnaby Village Museum

The Village Museum recreates early 20th century Burnaby with costumed interpreters, a farmhouse, a main street with shops, a blacksmith forge, and the C.W. Parker Carousel. It is **open seasonally**, and the **summer season ran May 2 to Sept. 7, 2026**. That means a regular October visit finds it closed. The next windows posted on its Hours and Rates page:

- **Haunted Village:** Oct. 23 to 25 and Oct. 30 to 31, 2026, 4 to 9 p.m. The museum says admission is free except for Haunted Village, so check its site for Haunted Village tickets. The carousel is closed during Haunted Village.
- **Festive Village:** Wednesday to Sunday, Nov. 25 to Dec. 13, 2 to 9 p.m., then daily Dec. 16, 2026 to Jan. 3, 2027, 2 to 9 p.m., closed Dec. 24 and 25.

Last admission is 30 minutes before closing. Pets are not allowed on site except service dogs.

## Rules and park honesty

- **No swimming.** The City says water quality makes Deer Lake suitable for secondary contact, like paddling, but not for swimming.
- **Paddling.** Canoes, kayaks, and paddleboards can launch from the dock and beach area off Sperling Avenue. The City notes a watercraft rental runs from the dock. This guide does not list rental hours or prices. Check with the operator.
- **Fishing** is allowed from the dock, the shore, or a belly boat with a valid BC freshwater fishing licence.
- **Dogs** must be leashed everywhere, including trails, and are not allowed on the beach or in the picnic area.
- **Bikes** are not permitted on the wooden boardwalks.
- **Barbecues.** Burnaby's Be Park Smart page says propane barbecues are allowed in all parks, and open-flame barbecues are allowed at Deer Lake Park only when permitted. Campfires are not allowed.
- **Trail surfaces** mix fine gravel, hard-packed dirt, and boardwalk. Some paths are steep and roots can trip you, so wear shoes with grip.
