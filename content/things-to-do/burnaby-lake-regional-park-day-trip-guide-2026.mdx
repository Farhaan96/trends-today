**Is Burnaby Lake a good fall day trip?** Yes, if you want a flat, quiet walk with real birdwatching and no big climbs. Metro Vancouver describes Burnaby Lake Regional Park as "water, woodlands, and wildlife" with level walking trails. For October 2026 the key facts are simple. **From Oct. 6 to Nov. 2 the park is open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m.** **Feeding wildlife is no longer allowed at Piper Spit**, or anywhere else in the park. The **Nature House is closed for the season**. Bikes are allowed on only two trails, and dogs must stay leashed and off Piper Spit. By transit, **Sperling-Burnaby Lake Station** on the Millennium Line sits at Sperling Avenue and Lougheed Highway. Use TransLink Trip Planner for the walk to the entrance you want.

## Why Burnaby Lake works this weekend

Thanksgiving weekend in Metro Vancouver usually means people looking for a walk that is close, free, and not a mountain. Burnaby Lake checks all three. It sits in the middle of Burnaby, the trails are level, and it is near SkyTrain. Metro Vancouver's own park page leads with the level walking trails, and its trail notes keep using the same word: flat.

The other draw is birds. Metro Vancouver says Cottonwood Trail runs through deciduous forest and thick shrub areas "which hide the lake but are great for looking for birds," with lake views at **Piper Spit** and **Phillips Point**. In fall, that mix of marsh, cottonwoods, and second growth forest is what makes it a good colour and wildlife walk. Colour timing changes every year with the weather, so treat any peak date you see online as a guess.

The fall hours are the practical reason to go now. From Oct. 6 to Nov. 2 the park stays open until 7 p.m. On Nov. 3 that drops to 6 p.m., and the brochure notes that gates are locked when the park is closed. That matters if you park inside and plan to stay for the late light.

If you already walked [Deer Lake Park](/things-to-do/deer-lake-park-burnaby-day-trip-guide-2026) this fall, Burnaby Lake is the bigger, wilder neighbour. Keep them as separate days.

## How to get there (transit first)

TransLink lists **Sperling-Burnaby Lake Station** on the Millennium Line, on the southeast corner of Sperling Avenue and Lougheed Highway in Burnaby. The Metro Vancouver park map also shows **Lake City Way** and **Production Way-University** stations near the park, and it marks bus stops around the edges.

The City of Burnaby's park page sends transit riders to **TransLink Trip Planner** rather than naming a route, so this guide does the same. Enter "4519 Piper Ave, Burnaby" as your destination for the Piper Avenue Entrance and Nature House area. This guide does not invent walk times or train frequencies. Check Trip Planner the day you go, especially if you are travelling on the holiday Monday, Oct. 12.

Arriving by bike? Metro Vancouver says cycling is not allowed on the north side trails, so there is a **bike rack near the Nature House** at the Piper Ave Entrance. Cyclists following the Central Valley Greenway are asked to use Cariboo Road and Winston Avenue rather than cutting through the park.

## Driving and parking honesty

The City of Burnaby says there is parking at the **Piper Avenue Entrance**. The Metro Vancouver park map also shows parking at the **Avalon Avenue** and **Glencarin** entrances. The brochure says horse trailers unload at the Avalon Avenue lot, so expect some equestrian traffic there.

The City notes the park has **wheelchair accessible parking spots and washrooms**. This guide does not list parking rates or stall counts because none of the pages checked today post them. Trust the signs at the lot.

The bigger parking rule is the clock. Park hours are posted at the entrances, and gates are locked when the park closes. For October that is 7 p.m., so plan to be back at your car before then.

## Half-day outline

1. **Start at Piper Avenue.** This is where the Nature House, toilets, boardwalk, viewing tower, and bike rack are. Even with the Nature House closed for the season, it is the easiest place to orient yourself with the posted map.
2. **Walk out to Piper Spit.** The boardwalk and viewing tower give you the open lake view the north side trails mostly hide. Leave the birdseed at home. Metro Vancouver says significant changes in wildlife behaviour showed feeding was doing more harm than good, so it is no longer allowed here.
3. **Pick a trail length.** For a medium walk, take **Cottonwood Trail**, 5.2 km return from the Piper Avenue Entrance, with lake access at Phillips Point. For something shorter with river views, take **Brunette Headwaters Trail**, 3.6 km return from either the Piper Avenue or Avalon Avenue entrance. It is flat, with stairs at Cariboo Dam, and connects to the **Conifer** and **Spruce** loop trails through second growth forest.
4. **Picnic, if the weather holds.** The park map marks picnic areas. Metro Vancouver's fire rating notice says charcoal and propane barbecues and gas stoves are only allowed in areas with picnic tables, and you must stay with them at all times.
5. **Leave before 7 p.m.** That is the October closing time, and gates lock.

If you want to go farther, the park map shows the **Southshore Trail** along the lake's south side and a full loop around the lake made of connected trails. Use the distance markers on the posted map to size it before you commit.

## Trails, bikes, and horses

Metro Vancouver is specific about who can use which trail.

- **Freeway Trail and Avalon Trail** are open for hiking, horseback riding, and cycling. Pedestrians yield to horses. Cyclists yield to both pedestrians and horses.
- **Every other trail is pedestrian-only**, except those signed as hiking and equestrian.
- Stay on designated trails to limit erosion and habitat damage.

The brochure adds that riders should stay to the centre of the trail because the soft edges can be hazardous.

## Accessibility details

Metro Vancouver gives more trail access detail than most park pages, so here it is in one place:

- At the **Piper Avenue Entrance**, the Nature House, toilets, boardwalk, and viewing tower are wheelchair accessible.
- **Cottonwood Trail** is 2.6 km long one way, with minimal slopes, a semi-firm surface, and some loose gravel. There are no stairs from Still Creek Bridge to Piper Spit, and the trail is at least 2 m wide.
- **Brunette Headwaters Trail** is 1.8 km long with minimal grade changes and a semi-firm surface. The slope along most of it is under 5 per cent.
- **Avalon Trail** is 0.8 km, firm rock dust, and at least 3 m wide.
- **Southshore Trail** is 3.4 km with minimal grade changes, but it has several boardwalks with multiple steps. Metro Vancouver says it is accessible for about 500 m west of the Avalon Trail junction.
- **Pavilion Trail** has stairs at the bridge over Deer Lake Brook.

## Dogs and wildlife

Dogs are welcome but must be **leashed and under control at all times**. They are **not allowed at Piper Spit**, to protect bird habitat. If your dog needs a run, the brochure points to the off-leash area at nearby **Warner Loat Park**.

Metro Vancouver also says **bears, cougars, and coyotes** may pass through the park from time to time. If you meet one, give it space, stay calm, and slowly back away. The brochure asks you to stay 100 m back from bears and not leave garbage.

## Fall rules worth knowing

- **No mushroom picking.** The brochure says vegetation and mushroom removal is prohibited to protect park habitats. That is worth remembering in October.
- **No liquor, camping, or cannabis.** Smoking is only allowed in designated areas marked by signs.
- **Canoeing is not recommended for novices.** The brochure says the marshy shoreline has few safe landing spots. Canoes can launch from Piper Spit, but check water levels first.
- **Rental properties in the park are closed to the public.** Respect the tenants' privacy.
- **Wildlife Rescue Association.** The brochure notes this rehabilitation facility for orphaned or injured wildlife is at the Glencarin Entrance.

## Events and the Nature House

The **Burnaby Lake Nature House is closed for the season**, according to Metro Vancouver. The brochure says it normally opens weekends and statutory holidays from mid-May through Labour Day.

Metro Vancouver's park page lists upcoming events, including a **Piper Spit Bird Count on Tuesday, Oct. 13, 9 to 11 a.m.**, the day after Thanksgiving, and another on **Nov. 3, 10 a.m. to noon**. Later listings include **The Nature of Crows** on Nov. 28 and a **Holiday Open House** on Dec. 6. Check the event listing for registration details before you go.

Metro Vancouver is also developing a **20-year management plan** for the park, and its page invites the public to help shape it.
