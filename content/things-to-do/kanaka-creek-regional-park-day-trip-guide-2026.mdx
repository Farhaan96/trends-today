**Is Kanaka Creek a good late October day trip?** Yes, if you want a short canyon walk, a working fish fence, and a flat riverfront in Maple Ridge. Metro Vancouver says Kanaka Creek provides habitat for a variety of wildlife, including salmon, and that people can explore the trails on foot, by bicycle, or on horseback, and visit the hatchery. For late October 2026 the planning facts are these. **The park is open 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. until November 2**, then **7 a.m. to 6 p.m. from November 3**. **Canyon Trail is 1.2 km one way** from the hatchery to Cliff Falls. **Riverfront trails are 3 km one way**. And a **bear safety reminder** is posted. It is an alert, not a closure of the main trails.

## Why Kanaka Creek works in late October

The two walks do different jobs. Metro Vancouver says Canyon Trail follows unique sandstone canyons with falls on the north and south arms of Kanaka Creek. It also says Riverfront trails are relatively flat, with outstanding wildlife viewing at the oxbow and the Fraser River.

The salmon timing is the other reason to go now. The September 2025 brochure says the fish fence operates from the end of September to the beginning of December. Fish are caught, counted, and the majority are released to spawn in the creek. The brochure asks visitors not to disturb fish at the fence. Late October sits inside that window. This guide does not claim how many fish will be at the fence on a given day.

## Getting there

**Driving.** Use the Google map link on the park page. This guide does not invent a street address or a parking rate. The brochure says to watch for authorized vehicles doing dyke or park maintenance on the Fraser Riverfront.

**Transit.** The Metro Vancouver park page does not list a transit option, and this guide does not invent one.

**Parking and the canoe launch.** The park page says canoes and kayaks carried by vehicles must be portaged from the parking lot to the canoe launch, a distance of 130 metres. There is a canoe launch at the Fraser Riverfront.

## Half-day outline

1. **Start at the Bell-Irving Hatchery.** The park page says the hatchery and fish fence support enhancement for chum and coho salmon and for cutthroat trout in Kanaka Creek. The September 2025 brochure describes monitoring of chum, coho, and pink salmon, and outdoor exhibits at the Kanaka Creek Watershed Stewardship Centre.
2. **Walk Canyon Trail if you want the falls.** It is 1.2 km one way, about 30 minutes, with some challenging hills. Stay back from cliff edges and keep out of the creek and canyon at Cliff Falls. The page says steep cliffs, slippery slopes, hidden underwater hazards and deceptive pool depths make rescue difficult.
3. **Or walk the riverfront.** Riverfront trails total 3 km one way on a relatively flat surface, with views of the oxbow and the Fraser River. The brochure says swimming in the river is not recommended because the water is fast flowing, murky and unsafe. The park page lists no swimming among its features.
4. **Paddle only the lower creek.** The page says the lower section at the Fraser Riverfront is suitable for canoeing and kayaking, and that upper reaches above the falls are not suitable.
5. **Leave before the gate.** The brochure says gates are locked when the park is closed. On Oct. 21 the live hours were 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. through November 2.

## Current alerts

**Bear safety reminder.** On Oct. 21, 2026, the Metro Vancouver park alerts page listed one Kanaka Creek notice, rated low. It says bears are active and looking for food, and that they are generally uninterested in people. It asks visitors to make noise, keep children close, keep pets leashed, stay at least 100 metres away (it says that is about 10 bus lengths), give bears an escape route, and remove garbage and food. If you see a bear at a distance, stay back and choose another route. The notice says negative encounters may result in closure of some areas or harm to the bear. It does not close Canyon Trail, the riverfront, or the hatchery.

**Fire rating.** The park page showed a moderate rating when we checked. At that rating, the page says charcoal and propane barbecues and gas cooking stoves are only allowed in areas with picnic tables, that you must stay with them, and that charcoal goes in the designated receptacles. Report wildfires to 911. The rating can change. Check it the morning you go.

## Dogs, wildlife and park rules

- Dogs must be leashed and under control at all times. Scoop the poop. The September 2025 brochure says even a well trained dog can frighten or injure wildlife and visitors.
- The park page says the park is habitat for bears, cougars and coyotes. Never approach or feed a bear. If you meet one, do not run: face it, back away slowly, and leave it a clear exit.
- Cycling is not a free-for-all. The brochure permits cycling on the Fraser Riverfront east of the Spit Trail and on the Trans Canada Trail. Horseback riding is listed on the Les Robson and North Fork Loop, Turkey Trot, Lower Thorn Hill, and parts of the Trans Canada Trail. Stay on designated trails. On shared trails, the brochure says pedestrians yield to equestrians, and cyclists yield to pedestrians and equestrians.
- Vegetation and mushroom removal is prohibited. The brochure says to take only photographs.
- Camping, liquor and cannabis are not permitted. Smoking is not permitted except in designated areas marked by signs, and those areas allow tobacco and e-cigarettes, not cannabis.
- Fishing downstream of the railway bridge needs a Tidal Water Sport Fishing Licence. Fishing elsewhere in the park needs a Non-tidal Angling Licence. The brochure lists Fisheries and Oceans Canada at 1-877-535-7307 and the provincial freshwater line at 1-877-855-3222. Confirm the current rules before you fish. This guide does not restate licence conditions.

## Accessibility

Metro Vancouver says the washrooms and some picnic tables are accessible at the Fraser Riverfront. The hatchery is accessible with assistance because some gravel areas are hard to manoeuvre. The pit toilet along the Nature Trail at the riverfront is partially accessible (a lip at the entrance, and the path surface). The pit toilets at the hatchery are also partially accessible (a lip, and no access path). Cliff Falls are not wheelchair accessible.
