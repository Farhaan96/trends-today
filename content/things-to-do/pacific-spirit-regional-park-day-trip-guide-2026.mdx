**Can you do a Pacific Spirit Regional Park day trip from Vancouver without a car?** Yes. Use [TransLink Trip Planner](https://www.translink.ca/) for your door-to-park trip. **#25** and **#33** stop on **West 16th Avenue at Pacific Spirit Park** on the way to UBC Exchange. Walk forest trails, the park centre, or Camosun Bog boardwalk. Prefer the bus on sunny weekends when roadside pull-offs fill. Confirm Trip Planner times and **posted park hours** at the entrance before you leave.

## Why Pacific Spirit works as a half day

Pacific Spirit is Metro Vancouver's busiest regional park by visits. It is a real forest next to UBC, not a downtown lawn. You can ride a West 16th Avenue bus, walk under canopy, and ride home without gambling on a scarce stall.

That is the point of this planner: one park, clear transit, trail and dog-sign honesty.

Set expectations. Trails get muddy after rain. Beach access trails (except Acadia Beach) are steep. Swim at your own risk. There are **no lifeguards**. This guide does **not** invent cafe hours on campus. Confirm those directly if they matter to your plan.

If you liked the forest headland of [Lighthouse Park by bus](/things-to-do/lighthouse-park-day-trip-guide-2026) or the West Side beach corridor of [Jericho + Kitsilano](/things-to-do/jericho-kitsilano-beach-day-trip-guide-2026), this is the closer Point Grey forest sibling by UBC buses. For a waterfront contrast later, pair with [Ambleside](/things-to-do/ambleside-park-west-vancouver-day-trip-guide-2026) or [Stanley Park](/things-to-do/stanley-park-day-trip-guide-2026).

## How to get there without a car

### West 16th Avenue to the park centre (preferred)

[TransitDB stop 51598](http://www.transitdb.ca/stop/51598/) lists these routes at **Westbound W 16 Ave @ Pacific Spirit Park**:

- **#25** King Edward / To UBC Exchange
- **#33** 16th and 33rd Avenue / To UBC Exchange

Alight at the Pacific Spirit Park stop on West 16th Avenue, then walk to the park centre and trailheads. Use [TransLink Trip Planner](https://www.translink.ca/) for the return eastbound stop across the street. Treat door-to-park times as **typical**, not guaranteed. UBC class peaks and weekend crowds change the run. Build a buffer.

### UBC Exchange, then walk

Many Broadway and University Boulevard buses terminate at **UBC Exchange**. From there you can walk into the park on the campus edge. That works well if Trip Planner prefers a 99 B-Line or other UBC trip for your start point. Still confirm the last walk segment. Do not invent a fixed minute count from your couch.

### Bike and shared trails

More than 34 km of trail is multi-use. Metro Vancouver's trail courtesy code: walkers yield to horses, and cyclists yield to both walkers and horses. Stay on mapped trails. Off-trail use causes erosion and damages habitat.

## Driving and parking (if you skip transit)

Prefer the bus so the half day is not a parking hunt. Roadside and trailhead pull-offs near popular entries fill early in good weather.

UBC campus paid visitor parking sits next to many trailheads. Confirm live rates and lot hours on [UBC Parking](https://parking.ubc.ca/) before you go. This guide does **not** invent hourly prices. If a free short-stay pull-off is posted on site, obey the posted time limit and signs.

Park gates lock when the park is closed. Do not leave a car inside a gated lot past posted hours.

## Hour-by-hour half-day outline

1. **Morning bus.** Ride **#25** or **#33** west on 16th Avenue toward UBC. Alight at Pacific Spirit Park.
2. **Park centre and short loop (60-90 minutes).** Use washrooms, pick a mapped trail from the park centre, and stay on signed paths. Soft, wide multi-use trails are the easy first choice in wet weather.
3. **Optional Camosun Bog block (30-45 minutes).** Boardwalk access from **19th Avenue**. Stay on the boardwalk. Keep dogs on-leash there. Treat the bog as fragile habitat.
4. **Optional beach edge (45-75 minutes).** Acadia Beach is the flatter beach access. Other beach trails are steep. No lifeguards. Carry litter out. Note clothing-optional areas on parts of the foreshore per Metro Vancouver brochure (Acadia Beach itself is the exception). Check signs.
5. **Return bus.** Walk back to West 16th Avenue with buffer before evening plans. Confirm eastbound Trip Planner.

Swap blocks if you arrive via UBC Exchange first. The forest is still a short walk from campus edges.

## What to expect on the trails

Metro Vancouver's brochure describes lush forest trails on foot, bike, or horse, steep trails down to scenic natural beaches, and the fragile **Camosun Bog** boardwalk. Accessible washrooms and picnic tables are at the **16th Avenue park centre** and **Clinton Meadow**.

**Ecological Reserve No. 74** is not open to the public. Environmentally sensitive areas need special permits. Stay on the public trail map.

Rules called out in the brochure: no liquor, no camping, no fires, no harvesting (mushrooms, fiddleheads, other vegetation). Smoking is not permitted except in designated areas marked by signs. Swim at your own risk.

Area office for Pacific Spirit: **604-224-5739**. Park conditions are posted on metrovancouver.org. Emergencies: **9-1-1**.

## Dogs, beaches, and signs

Dogs are welcome on many Pacific Spirit trails. Watch for **Canine Code of Conduct** and **trail designation** signs. Designations exist to protect habitat and reduce conflict. Scoop and dispose of waste properly.

**Acadia Beach to Trail 6** brochure window:

- **No dogs** March 1 to September 30
- **Leash optional** October 1 to February 28

On **September 30**, treat that beach stretch as **no dogs**. From **October 1**, leash-optional season starts on that stretch per the brochure map note. Confirm the live trailhead sign the morning you go. Rules elsewhere in the park still follow posted designations.

Do not invent a blanket off-leash park. Read the sign at each junction.

## Food and cafes

This guide does **not** quote UBC cafe or food-truck hours. Pack water. If you want campus food after the walk, confirm operator hours the morning you go.

## Weather, crowds, and honesty

Pacific Spirit sees heavy use. Sunny weekends and UBC term peaks mean busier trailheads and fuller nearby parking. For quieter trails, go earlier on a shoulder day and arrive by bus.

Fall is strong for cooler forest walks, but mud and early dusk matter. Plan to be at your return stop with bus buffer before you are tired and cold. Earliest park closing is **5 p.m.**; many days close later, but only the posted entrance hours count.
