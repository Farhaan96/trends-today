Fall colour walks near Vancouver are mostly free. The practical constraints are daylight, mud, and park hours, not admission fees.

## Strong free and low-cost options include Pacific Spirit, Stanley Park paths, Burnaby Mountain, Deer Lake, Maplewood Flats, and Minnekhada. Gold Creek Falls in Golden Ears is a longer drive but does not require a day-use pass after Sept. 7. Wear grippy shoes; finish before dark; and confirm park notices before you leave.

This guide covers in-city walks, Burnaby and Coquitlam trails, North Shore options, and one Fraser Valley add-on. Each entry includes parking, transit, and timing notes verified against official sources.

---

## How to use this list

Pick based on your available time, not just distance. A 30-minute transit trip to Pacific Spirit may be easier than driving to Golden Ears on a weekend morning. Check the weather forecast: trails get muddy after rain, and some boardwalk sections can be slippery.

Peak fall colour in the Lower Mainland typically runs from mid-October through early November, though timing varies by elevation and year. September walks are pleasant but show early colour only. If you want peak foliage, aim for Thanksgiving weekend or the two weeks after.

Some parks close gates at dusk. Plan to finish with daylight to spare.

## In-city colour walks

### Pacific Spirit Regional Park

Pacific Spirit is the largest forested park on Vancouver's west side, with over 70 km of trails managed by Metro Vancouver Regional Parks. The Admiralty Trail near Spanish Banks is the fall colour highlight: a tunnel of bigleaf maples that turn gold and orange in October.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: Admiralty Trail is about 1.5 hours return. The full park has trails ranging from 2 km to 12 km.
- **Difficulty**: Easy. Mostly flat with some gentle hills.
- **Parking**: Free parking areas on NW Marine Drive near Spanish Banks, and at various trailheads along 16th Avenue.
- **Transit**: Several TransLink bus routes serve the park, including the 4, 44, and 84.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on most trails. Some areas require leashes; off-leash sections are marked.
- **Hours**: Dawn to dusk. No gates.

The Salish Trail (5 km loop) through old-growth hemlock and Douglas fir is another option, though it stays green year-round.

[Metro Vancouver Pacific Spirit Regional Park map (PDF)](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/Documents/pacific-spirit-regional-park-map.pdf)

### Stanley Park and Lost Lagoon

Stanley Park is Vancouver's most visited park. The Lost Lagoon loop is a flat 1.8 km walk that takes about 30 minutes and is fully accessible.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: Lost Lagoon loop is 1.8 km. Seawall is 9 km for the full loop.
- **Difficulty**: Easy. Paved paths and some gravel.
- **Parking**: Pay parking throughout the park. Check current rates.
- **Transit**: Take the 19 bus from downtown. The park entrance is a short walk from Georgia Street.
- **Dogs**: Must be leashed.
- **Fall colour timing**: Peak colour typically mid-October through early November.

Vine maples turn crimson and bigleaf maples go golden along the trails. The area around Lost Lagoon, Beaver Lake, and the Japanese Canadian War Memorial are particularly colourful.

[City of Vancouver Lost Lagoon walk guide](https://vancouver.ca/parks-recreation-culture/stanley-park-lost-lagoon.aspx)

---

### Queen Elizabeth Park note

Queen Elizabeth Park at Cambie and 33rd Avenue is a good urban option with panoramic views, but it is more manicured garden than forest trail. Pay parking applies. The Canada Line's King Edward Station is nearby.

Fall colour here peaks in mid to late October, especially along the upper walkways near Seasons in the Park restaurant. The Bloedel Conservatory charges admission but offers indoor warmth if the weather turns.

## Burnaby and Coquitlam

### Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area

Burnaby Mountain has 30 km of trails through 576 hectares of conservation land. The views of Burrard Inlet and the North Shore mountains reward the climb.

**What to know:**

- **Difficulty**: Varies. Some trails are steep and technical. Pipeline Trail and Trans-Canada Trail sections are easier.
- **Parking**: Free parking along Centennial Way, at Harry Jerome Sports Centre off Barnet Road, and at the Mountain Air Bike Skills lot on Takeda Drive. Limited street parking at Hastings Street and Ridgeview Drive. Main lot closes at 10 p.m.
- **Transit**: Take the 145 to SFU, then walk down.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on leash.
- **Current closure**: The Velodrome Trail stairs are closed indefinitely due to erosion. Check the City of Burnaby site before planning a route that includes this trail.

The picnic areas near Simon Fraser University have been closed intermittently due to bear activity. Check [City of Burnaby parks updates](https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/burnaby-mountain-conservation-area) before your visit.

---

### Deer Lake Park

Deer Lake is one of Burnaby's most accessible parks for fall colour. Maples and willows along the shoreline turn gold and orange, reflected in the lake.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: The lake loop is about 5 km. Shorter routes are available.
- **Difficulty**: Easy. Flat trails with gravel paths and wooden boardwalks.
- **Parking**: Free parking at several lots. Access from Canada Way or Sperling Avenue.
- **Transit**: Take the SkyTrain to Metrotown, then the 144 bus. Or walk from Sperling-Burnaby Lake Station.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on leash.

The boardwalk sections offer the best fall reflection photography. The park connects to the Shadbolt Centre for the Arts, Burnaby Art Gallery, and Burnaby Village Museum if you want to extend your outing.

[City of Burnaby Deer Lake Park trails map](https://www.burnaby.ca/explore-outdoors/parks/deer-lake-park)

### Minnekhada Regional Park

Minnekhada is a 211-hectare Metro Vancouver regional park in northeast Coquitlam. The High Knoll viewpoint (5 km round trip) overlooks Pitt River and the surrounding marsh.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: Total trail network is about 10 km. High Knoll is roughly 5 km return.
- **Difficulty**: Moderate. Some steep sections with stone steps.
- **Parking**: Free at Quarry Road and Oliver Road entrances. Quarry Road fills first on weekends; arrive before 10 a.m.
- **Transit**: Limited. Driving is the practical option.
- **Hours**: Currently 7 a.m. to 8 p.m. (Sept. 3-23). Hours shorten to 7 a.m. to 7 p.m. starting Sept. 24, then 7 a.m. to 5 p.m. from Nov. 3.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on leash.
- **No cycling** on park trails. Bikes permitted only on Oliver Road and Pitt River dykes.
- **Current note**: Cedar Drive between 4150 Cedar Drive and Gilleys Trail is closed until fall 2027 for construction. Expect detours and delays on routes to the park.

The marsh is excellent for birdwatching. Minnekhada Lodge opens to the public on the first Sunday of the month, 1 to 4 p.m., February through December.

[Minnekhada Park Association visitor information](https://minnekhada.ca/plan-your-visit/)

## North Shore

### Maplewood Flats Conservation Area

Maplewood Flats is a 126-hectare tidal marsh and bird sanctuary in North Vancouver, managed by the Wild Bird Trust of BC. The 4 km loop is flat and easy, with views over the marsh.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: About 4 km loop.
- **Difficulty**: Easy. Gravel paths.
- **Parking**: Free at 2649 Dollarton Highway. Do not park in Environment Canada staff spots during weekday business hours.
- **Transit**: TransLink bus stops on Dollarton Highway near the entrance. Check [TransLink fall service changes](/transit/translink-fall-service-changes-september-2026) for current schedules.
- **Hours**: Open 24 hours. Office hours Monday to Friday, 9 a.m. to 5 p.m.
- **Dogs**: Not allowed.
- **Washrooms**: Available when volunteers staff the nature house (typically weekends and some weekdays).

This is a birding destination more than a fall foliage spot, but the marsh grasses turn golden in autumn. The second Saturday of each month at 10 a.m. features a free guided nature walk (donations encouraged).

[Wild Bird Trust Maplewood Flats visitor guide](https://wildbirdtrust.org/visit-maplewood-flats/)

---

### Quarry Rock, Deep Cove

Quarry Rock is Metro Vancouver's most popular short hike: a 3.8 km return trail to a granite outcropping with views over Indian Arm. Fall colour peaks along the Baden-Powell Trail in October.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: 3.8 km return.
- **Difficulty**: Easy to moderate. About 100 m elevation gain.
- **Time**: 1 to 1.5 hours return.
- **Parking**: Seasonal pay parking at Panorama Park and Deep Cove Park lots, $3/hour from 8 a.m. to 6 p.m. (March 1 to Oct. 31). Lots fill by 9 a.m. on weekends. Overflow parking on Rockliff Road and at Myrtle Park.
- **Transit**: Take the 211 or 212 bus from Phibbs Exchange. Total trip from downtown is 50 to 60 minutes. Note: seasonal service on these routes is being rolled back effective Sept. 7. Check TransLink for current schedules.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on leash.

The trail passes through old-growth forest before reaching the granite summit. The village has cafes for post-hike refuelling.

## Fraser Valley add-on

### Gold Creek Falls, Golden Ears Provincial Park

Gold Creek Falls is a 15-minute walk from the Gold Creek parking lot in Golden Ears Provincial Park. The trail passes through coastal western hemlock forest with views of the creek.

**What to know:**

- **Distance**: Short walk from parking, about 30 minutes return.
- **Difficulty**: Easy. Gradual hill.
- **Parking**: Free day-use pass was required May 15 to Sept. 7. After Sept. 7, no pass required for the fall season.
- **Hours**: Park gates open 7 a.m. to 11 p.m. until mid-October. Starting mid-October, gates close at 7 p.m.
- **Drive time**: About 56 minutes from Vancouver via Trans-Canada Highway.
- **Dogs**: Allowed on leash.

The waterfall is most dramatic in spring, but the surrounding forest is colourful in fall. Check [BC Parks Golden Ears trail conditions (PDF)](https://bcparks.ca/golden-ears-park/) before heading out, as trails have been impacted by recent storms.

## Packing and safety for fall

Fall weather in the Lower Mainland can shift quickly. Pack layers and be prepared for rain.

**Essentials:**

- **Grippy shoes**: Trails get muddy and slippery after rain. Hiking shoes or boots with tread are better than runners.
- **Layers**: Morning walks can be cool; afternoons warm up. Bring a light jacket you can tie around your waist.
- **Rain gear**: A packable rain jacket is useful even on forecast-clear days.
- **Water and snacks**: Shorter trails do not require much, but bring water.
- **Daylight**: Check sunset times. Finish trails with time to spare. As of late September, sunset is around 7 p.m. and getting earlier.
- **Phone charge**: Cell coverage is spotty in some parks. Download offline maps if using your phone for navigation.

Bears are active in some parks, particularly Burnaby Mountain and Minnekhada. Make noise on trails and check park notices for closures.
