**Can you do a Stanley Park day trip from Vancouver without a car?** Yes. Take TransLink bus **#19** into the park. City of Vancouver directions list stops at **Stanley Park Drive and Pipeline Road** near Lost Lagoon, and at **Stanley Park Loop** near the Miniature Train (a short walk from the Vancouver Aquarium area). Park hours are **6 a.m. to 10 p.m.** unless otherwise posted. Walk the seawall one way, **counter-clockwise**, about **10 km** for the full loop. Prefer the bus on sunny weekends. If you drive, EasyPark Lot 62 pay parking applies year-round on all park roadways: through **Sept. 30, 2026** the posted rate is **$4.75 per hour** (daily maximum **$17.75**). Confirm Trip Planner times and EasyPark fees before you leave. There is **no** bus service around the park loop itself.

## Why Stanley Park works as a half day (or full day)

Stanley Park is a **400-hectare** West Coast rainforest peninsula next to downtown Vancouver. City materials highlight scenic trails, beaches, waterfront views along the seawall, wildlife viewing, and cultural and natural landmarks. You do not need a paid attraction ticket to walk the seawall or forest paths.

That is the point of this planner: land by bus, walk a seawall section or a forest loop, and get home without gambling on a scarce stall. You can stretch it into a full day if you add Prospect Point, Brockton, or a beach stop. You can also keep it to a calm two or three hours.

Set expectations. The seawall is popular. Sunny weekends are crowded. There is no bus that circles the park for you. Once the #19 drops you, you are walking or biking.

If you liked the forest loops of [Lighthouse Park](/things-to-do/lighthouse-park-day-trip-guide-2026) or the False Creek energy of [Granville Island](/things-to-do/granville-island-day-trip-guide-2026), Stanley Park is the downtown sibling that still works without a car.

## How to get there without a car

### Bus #19 (preferred)

Take TransLink bus **#19** (Kingsway / Stanley Park). City of Vancouver directions say the bus will get you into Stanley Park and lists these stops:

- **Stanley Park Drive and Pipeline Road** near Lost Lagoon
- **Stanley Park Loop** near the Miniature Train, a short walk from the Vancouver Aquarium area

Stanley Park Loop is the common turnaround. Arrival at the loop is often drop-off oriented; board back toward Metrotown or downtown from the loop bays Trip Planner shows for your return. Treat stop layouts and headways as live data, not a promise from this page.

If you arrive by **SeaBus**, City directions say you can take a short walk to transfer to the **#19**, or walk or bike the waterfront Coal Harbour route into the park.

### No transit around the park loop

City directions are clear: **there is no public transit service around the park**. Do not expect a hop-on bus every few viewpoints. Plan your seawall distance on foot (or bring a bike) and leave return buffer for the #19.

### Confirm before you leave

Use [TransLink Trip Planner](https://www.translink.ca/) or the Transit app the morning you go. Door-to-park time from downtown is often short on a good day, but transfers, traffic on Georgia, and weekend crowds change that. Build slack.

## Driving and pay parking (if you skip transit)

Prefer the #19 so the half day is not a parking hunt. Pay parking is in effect **year-round** throughout Stanley Park, including **all roadways** inside the park. EasyPark manages the lots. Overnight parking is not allowed in Vancouver parks.

EasyPark **Lot 62 (Stanley Park)** detailed rates checked Sept. 24, 2026:

| Season window | Hourly | Daily maximum |
| --- | --- | --- |
| April 1 to September 30 | **$4.75** | **$17.75** |
| October 1 to March 31 | **$4.25** | **$12.75** |

Daily rates include GST and Parking Sales Tax. Daily passes are good for any lot or space in Stanley Park where daily tickets are sold. Some meters sell hourly only (City map marks those separately). Pay by meter or apps EasyPark lists (Zipby, Honk Mobile, Passport, or Park Mobile). Confirm the live EasyPark Lot 62 page before you go; rates can change.

Seasonal and annual vehicle passes exist for frequent visitors. This guide does not price those as a day-trip default. Check EasyPark if you visit often.

## Hour-by-hour half-day outline

Use this as a template, not a stopwatch.

1. **Late morning #19.** Board toward Stanley Park with slack. Aim for Stanley Park Loop if you want the Miniature Train / Aquarium side, or Pipeline Road if Lost Lagoon is the start.
2. **Orient and washroom.** Grab water. Note your return stop before you wander.
3. **Seawall section, not a forced full marathon.** A half day often means Coal Harbour to Brockton, or Second Beach to Siwash Rock, not the entire 10 km unless you want it. Travel **counter-clockwise**.
4. **Viewpoint or forest spur.** Prospect Point and Brockton are classic. Stay on marked paths.
5. **Food buffer.** Pack a snack, or confirm cafe hours the morning you go. This guide omits operator hours.
6. **Return #19.** Walk back to Pipeline Road or the Loop with bus buffer before evening plans. Park closes at **10 p.m.**

## Seawall honesty

City of Vancouver describes the Stanley Park seawall destination walk as about **10 km** (roughly 13,000 steps) around the park. Travel on the seawall is **one way, counter-clockwise**.

The wider Vancouver seawall system is split into clearly marked sections:

- Walkers and joggers nearest the water
- Cyclists and inline skaters on the inside path

Follow the signs, especially on sunny weekends. A full walk often takes about **2 to 3 hours**; cycling the loop is often cited around **1 hour** on City materials. Those are planning ranges, not your personal pace.

Check City seawall and park notices for temporary closures before you go. Do not invent a detour from a third-party hike app alone.

## Dogs, rules, and washrooms

Follow posted Park Board rules for dogs, bikes, and shared paths. Keep dogs under control and respect leash signs where posted.

Washrooms are available from **dawn until dusk** per City Stanley Park materials. Expect popular washrooms near major destinations to queue on sunny weekends.

Leave no trace. Pack out garbage. This is a shared urban forest and waterfront, not a private backyard.

## Food and cafes

Keep food framing casual. Pack water and a snack if you can. This guide **omits specific cafe and restaurant opening hours** because operator hours pages were not confirmed as primary sources morning of draft, and third-party aggregators do not count.

If you want coffee or a sit-down meal inside or next to the park, confirm venue hours the morning you go, or plan food downtown before or after the #19 ride.

## Weather, crowds, and honesty

Sunny weekends fill lots and the popular seawall stretches. If you want quieter photos, go earlier on a shoulder day and arrive by bus.

Rain makes pavement slick. Ocean wind at Prospect Point can feel colder than the downtown forecast, so pack a layer even on mild September days.

Fall is a strong season for forest colour and cooler walking, but it is not a reason to ignore fade-to-dark timing. The park closes at **10 p.m.** Plan to be at your return stop with bus buffer before you are tired and cold.
