**Can you do a Spanish Banks day trip from Vancouver by transit?** Yes. On weekends and holidays from **May to September**, TransLink's **Route 42 Spanish Banks** runs from Broadway and Alma about every 15 minutes (roughly 7 a.m. to 10 p.m. per TransLink's July 2025 Buzzer guide) and also serves Locarno and Jericho. The rest of the year, and on weekdays, take the **4** or **84** to **W 4th Avenue and Blanca Street** and walk about **15 minutes** down to the beach. Spanish Banks has three sections (East, West quiet beach, Extension with kite and off-leash zones) on the Seaside Seawall. If you drive, City of Vancouver seasonal parking (October through March as of the Oct. 1, 2026 banner) is **free for the first two hours**, then **$3 per additional hour**, with a possible app fee. EasyPark Lot 189 also lists **$4.25 per hour** for April 1 to September 30 (9 a.m. to 10 p.m.). Lots fill on sunny days, so prefer transit when you can. Confirm Trip Planner, lifeguard season, and concession status before you go.

## Why Spanish Banks works as a half day

Spanish Banks is the wide, sandy English Bay stretch west of Locarno and Jericho. The City of Vancouver describes three distinct sections along Northwest Marine Drive west of Tolmie Street: **Spanish Banks East**, **Spanish Banks West**, and **Spanish Banks Extension**. At low tide, the water can sit about one kilometre offshore. The Seaside Seawall ties the sections together. Views take in the North Shore mountains, downtown, and English Bay.

Set expectations early. Sunny peak weekends fill the lots and the walk corridor. Lifeguards are a **Victoria Day to Labour Day** window only. Fall and winter visits are walk, seawall, and picnic more than swim. That is fine for a half day if you dress for English Bay wind and check the tide.

If you already know [Jericho and Kitsilano](/things-to-do/jericho-kitsilano-beach-day-trip-guide-2026), Spanish Banks is the next seawall hop west: more sand flats, clearer section rules, and a transit plan that changes with the calendar.

## How to get there (transit first)

### Weekends and holidays, May to September (Route 42)

TransLink's Buzzer how-to (published July 16, 2025) is the clearest public guide. On **weekends and holidays between May and September**, ride the **42 Spanish Banks** from **Broadway and Alma Street**. Service is every **15 minutes**, between about **7 a.m. and 10 p.m.** The 42 also stops at Locarno Beach Park and Jericho Beach Park.

To reach Broadway and Alma, take the **99 B-Line** from Commercial-Broadway Station (Expo and Millennium Lines), or ride the **7**, **9**, or **14**. Outside that May-to-September weekend and holiday window, do not count on a direct beach bus. Use TransLink Trip Planner the morning you go.

### Weekdays and October to April (walk from 4th and Blanca)

For weekday visits and the rest of the year, the same Buzzer guide says it is about a **15-minute walk** from the **4 Powell/UBC** or **84 UBC/VCC-Clark** stop at **W 4th Avenue and Blanca Street**. By SkyTrain, connect to the 4 at **Waterfront Station** (Expo Line and SeaBus), and to the 84 at **Olympic Village Station** (Canada Line).

Label that walk time as typical, not door-to-door. The climb back up from the beach to 4th is the real cost of an off-season transit visit. This guide does not invent current headways. Check Trip Planner for your start point.

### Bike and seasonal Mobi

Spanish Banks sits on the Seawall greenway. The Buzzer notes a temporary **Mobi by Rogers** station at Spanish Banks in summer (late April through the end of September in the 2025 post), available 24/7 while that temporary station is in place. Treat Mobi as seasonal. Confirm the station map the morning you ride. Bike or transit keeps the half day from turning into a parking hunt.

## Driving and parking honesty

City of Vancouver's Spanish Banks page (checked Oct. 7, 2026) carries a **What's happening** banner: **seasonal parking rates begin October 1** and run **through March**. You can park **free for the first 2 hours each day**. After that, parking costs **$3 for each additional hour**. When paying through a third-party parking app, a **$0.45** transaction fee applies. There are no fees when paying at a meter.

**EasyPark** manages the park lots. EasyPark Lot 189 (Spanish Banks, 4707 NW Marine Drive), checked the same morning, lists:

- **October 1 to March 31**, Monday to Sunday and holidays, 9 a.m. to 10 p.m.: first two hours free, then **$3.00 per hour**
- **April 1 to September 30**, Monday to Sunday and holidays, 9 a.m. to 10 p.m.: **$4.25 per hour**

Lot 189 shows about **305** stalls. The City also notes some pay parking along **NW Marine Drive from Blanca Street to W 4th Avenue**. TransLink's Buzzer says there is limited paid parking and recommends transit or cycling to avoid the hunt. Rates and apps can change. Prefer what the meter and EasyPark show on the day you arrive.

## Hour-by-hour half-day outline

1. **Late morning transit.** In season on a weekend or holiday, take the 42 from Broadway and Alma. Otherwise take the 4 or 84 to 4th and Blanca and walk down.
2. **Seawall stroll.** Walk East to West or West to East. Keep the quiet-beach rule in mind when you hit West.
3. **Picnic or concession buffer.** City lists concessions at East and West. Confirm they are open the morning you go. Bring layers.
4. **Optional Extension loop.** If you have energy, continue to Extension for open sand, kite space, and the dog off-leash area.
5. **Return with slack.** Climb back to Blanca before evening, or catch the 42 while it is running. Do not invent a swim plan outside lifeguard season. For water quality, check Fraser Health recreational water notices rather than assuming it is fine.

Pair Locarno or Jericho only as a seawall add-on if you still have legs, not as a forced mega beach crawl after a full Spanish Banks loop. The [Jericho and Kitsilano day-trip guide](/things-to-do/jericho-kitsilano-beach-day-trip-guide-2026) covers that stretch on its own.

## East, West, and Extension (what each is for)

### Spanish Banks East

City features: Seaside Seawall, concession, public washrooms, barbeques permitted, picnic tables, eight volleyball courts, seasonal lifeguards and a water wheelchair (Victoria Day to Labour Day; water wheelchair needs two attendants). This is the default family section.

### Spanish Banks West

Same seawall, concession, washrooms, barbeques, picnic tables, eight volleyball courts, and seasonal lifeguards and water wheelchair. The difference that matters: West is a **designated quiet beach**. Amplified sound is not permitted. There is a dog off-leash area about **200 m west of the building**. Lead with the quiet-beach rule so nobody shows up with speakers.

### Spanish Banks Extension

Seawall, barbeques permitted, dog off-leash area, and a kiteboarding launch zone. Less of a concession beach day. More open sand and wind sports.

## Rules and beach honesty

The City note is blunt: **fishing or harvesting of bivalve mollusks** (clams, oysters, mussels) is **not permitted**. Low-tide flats are wide. Stay aware of the incoming tide and soft sand. Lifeguards are seasonal only on East and West. This guide does not invent swim-safe claims. Use Fraser Health beach water-quality notices when you care about swimming. Dress in layers for English Bay wind in fall.

## Food and cafes
