**Is Burnaby Mountain a good fall day trip?** Yes, if you want big views without leaving the city. The City of Burnaby describes Burnaby Mountain Conservation Area as about **576 hectares (1,423 acres)** next to Simon Fraser University, overlooking Burrard Inlet, with scenic views that are best at sunset, picnic and play areas, and forest trails. For mid-October 2026 the key facts are these. **The conservation area is open dawn to dusk year-round**, and **the main parking lot closes at 10 pm**. There are **34 multi-use trails over 28 km**. **The Velodrome trail stair section is closed indefinitely.** **The Kamui Mintara (Playground of the Gods) sculpture is fenced** while the City assesses it, so you view it from outside the fence. Burnaby Hospitality lists **Mintara restaurant as closed from October 2025 to fall 2026 for renovations**, so call before you count on a meal there. Fall colour depends on the weather, so treat any peak-date claim online as a guess.

## Why Burnaby Mountain works in mid-October

[Burnaby Lake](/things-to-do/burnaby-lake-regional-park-day-trip-guide-2026), [Deer Lake](/things-to-do/deer-lake-park-burnaby-day-trip-guide-2026), and [Central Park](/things-to-do/central-park-burnaby-day-trip-guide-2026) are the flat Burnaby walks. Burnaby Mountain is the one with height. The upper slope looks out over Burrard Inlet and the Vancouver skyline, and the City's own page singles out sunset as the time to bring a camera.

The forest on the slopes mixes deciduous and coniferous trees, which is why the City mentions the rich changing leaves of fall. That is a description of the season, not a peak date. If the leaves are turning, great. If not, the view from the meadow is still the reason to go.

Dusk matters here. The conservation area closes at dusk, and mid-October sunsets come earlier every day. If you want the sunset view, plan to be near the parking lot and the upper meadow, not deep on a forest trail, when the light goes.

## Getting there and parking

**Driving.** The City's directions are simple: from Lougheed Highway, turn north on Gaglardi Way to Centennial Way. The address is **100 Centennial Way**.

**Where to park.** Burnaby asks you to park in designated areas:

- Along Centennial Way toward the Centennial Rose Garden (the main lot, which closes at 10 pm)
- Harry Jerome Sports Centre off Barnet Road, if you are starting from the bottom of the mountain
- Limited street parking at Hastings Street and Ridgeview Drive

For trail hiking and biking, the trails page also points to the **Mountain Air Bike Skills parking lot** off Takeda Drive on the south side of Barnet Highway, which it says has more capacity than the Drummond's Walk trailhead lot in Barnet Marine Park. This guide does not invent parking rates or stall counts. Trust the signs at the lot.

**Transit.** The City's page links to TransLink Trip Planner rather than naming a route. This guide does the same. Set your destination to 100 Centennial Way, Burnaby, and check the day you go.

## Half-day outline

1. **Park at the Centennial Way lot.** It sits beside the Centennial Rose Garden and the upper picnic and play areas.
2. **Walk the upper meadow.** This is where the Kamui Mintara poles stand. The City describes more than a dozen carved wooden poles by Ainu sculptors Nuburi and his son Shusei Toko, a symbol of goodwill between Burnaby and its sister city Kushiro, Japan. Right now the sculpture is fenced, so enjoy it from outside.
3. **Stop at the Centennial Rose Garden.** Burnaby says it has more than 900 rose bushes. This guide does not promise blooms in mid-October.
4. **Take a short trail if you have time.** Pandora Trail starts just above the Centennial Way lot with views along the upper slope. The City warns it is one of the most difficult trails in the area because of steep grades and uneven surfaces, so wear real shoes.
5. **Be back near the lot before dusk.** That is the posted closing time for the conservation area.

## Trails: what is open and what is not

**Velodrome trail ("Mini Grind"): stairs closed.** The Velodrome Trail is a 1,400-metre route from Harry Jerome Sports Centre with a roughly 240 vertical metre climb, an average grade of 20 percent, and a 500-tread timber stairway. The City has **closed the entire stair area between Mountain Air Trail and the top of Pandora Trail** because of dirt erosion and aging wood steps. The closure is indefinite while staff plan the restoration. Do not plan your workout around the stairs this fall.

**Pandora Trail.** Starts just above the Centennial Way lot and runs along the upper slope before dropping into forest. The City calls it one of the most difficult trails on the mountain. Walking from the Centennial Pavilion back to the car park on Pandora takes about 25 minutes, depending on your pace, per the trails page.

**The 8.9 km loop.** For a longer day, the trails page describes an 8.9-kilometre loop using the Trans Canada Trail, Cougar Creek Trail, Barnet Trail, and Mountain Air Trail. Check the City trail map before you go, since the closed Velodrome stair section touches Mountain Air Trail and Pandora Trail.

**Trail etiquette.** Trails are shared with walkers, runners, hikers, and cyclists, and one trail allows horseback riding. Pedestrians have right of way. Keep right and pass on the left. If you wear headphones, keep the volume low enough to hear people and animals. Mountain bikers should ride only when the trail is dry. Stay on the trails and out of out-of-bounds areas.

**Weather.** The City says trail conditions can change quickly in severe weather, and in high winds you should leave the conservation area immediately. October storms are a real reason to check the forecast.

## Wildlife

Burnaby lists black-tailed deer, coyotes, bald eagles, and smaller animals on the mountain. It also says **black bears and cougars are occasional visitors** to the creeks and streams near the forest trails. Read the City's Wildlife in Burnaby page before you go so you know what to do if you see one, and keep your dog leashed.

## Accessibility

The City says the amenities near the Mintara parking lot are wheelchair accessible, as is the restaurant building. The forest trails are steep and rugged, so this guide does not call them accessible. If you use a mobility aid, plan your visit around the upper lot, the meadow edge, and the rose garden, and confirm details with the City at 604-294-7450.

## Seasonal honesty

**Mintara restaurant:** Burnaby Hospitality's restaurant page reads "Closed from October 2025 to fall 2026 for renovations." As of Oct. 16, 2026, it is not clear from that page whether the restaurant has reopened. Call 604-297-4888 (listed as Monday to Sunday, 10 am to 4 pm) before you plan lunch.

**Eco-sculpture:** The City says the plant-covered eco-sculpture is a spring and summer feature, so do not expect it in mid-October.

**Fall colour:** Variable. No official peak date is posted on the pages checked today.

## Dogs and park rules

- Dogs on leash at all times, especially on steep sections, out of streams and on the trails. Pick up after your dog.
- Smoking is banned in all City parks and multi-use paths since Jan. 1, 2022.
- Park only in designated areas.
