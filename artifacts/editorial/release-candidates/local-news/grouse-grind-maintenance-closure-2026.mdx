**The Grouse Grind is closed from October 13 to November 10 for annual maintenance work.** Metro Vancouver’s current alert directs hikers to stay off the trail during the closure, so visitors should not head to the trailhead expecting weekend access.

Metro Vancouver says the **BCMC Route remains open** and reaches the same destination, but it is a separate challenging trail. Check the live park alert before every trip in case conditions or access change.

---

## What is closed at Grouse Mountain?

The closure applies to the **Grouse Grind Trail** in Grouse Mountain Regional Park. Metro Vancouver describes the Grind as a **2.5-kilometre**, one-way climb with about **800 metres of elevation gain** and a challenging rating.

The agency’s [park alerts page](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park-alerts-and-notices) lists the closure from **October 13 to November 10** for annual maintenance work. The [Grouse Mountain Regional Park page](https://metrovancouver.org/services/regional-parks/park/grouse-mountain-regional-park) provides the route details hikers need to compare before choosing another trail.

Before leaving home:

- **Check the live alert**: The status can change as work progresses.
- **Do not enter the closed trail**: The closure applies every day of the week.
- **Plan a different route**: Do not assume the Grind will reopen for a weekend visit.

---

## Is the BCMC Route an alternative?

Metro Vancouver lists the **BCMC Route** as a separate trail in the same regional park. It is **2.9 kilometres** long, gains about **750 metres**, and is rated challenging. The park describes it as more rustic than the Grouse Grind, with both second-growth and old-growth forest.

That makes the BCMC a possible plan only for hikers prepared for a steep, rugged route. It is not a simple bypass around maintenance work, and Metro Vancouver says dogs are not allowed on either the Grouse Grind or the BCMC Route.

Compare the two routes before deciding:

- **Grouse Grind**: 2.5 km, 800 m gain, challenging and currently closed.
- **BCMC Route**: 2.9 km, 750 m gain, challenging and more rustic.
- **Baden-Powell section**: 1.1 km, 150 m gain, moderate and leash-required for dogs.

Use the current park page and posted signs to confirm which trails are open when you arrive. Conditions, hours and access can change independently of the Grind closure.

---

## What the downhill rule actually covers

[Grouse Mountain Resort’s Grouse Grind guidance](https://www.grousemountain.com/grousegrind) says downhill hiking is not permitted for people hiking up the Grouse Grind. Under normal Grind operations, those hikers buy a **Download Ticket** for the Red Skyride or Blue Grouse Gondola.

The page does not say that this rule applies to every other trail in the regional park. Anyone considering the BCMC Route should plan that route separately, follow Metro Vancouver’s current trail information and posted signs, and confirm mountain operations before leaving.

For future Grouse Grind visits after the trail reopens, the resort says the first daily download for Grind hikers is at **7:30 a.m.** Mountain access hours and operating conditions can change.

A safe trip plan should include:

- **A confirmed open route**: Verify it with Metro Vancouver.
- **A route-specific return plan**: Do not transfer the Grind’s rules to another trail without checking.
- **Weather-ready gear**: Metro Vancouver recommends proper footwear, water, food, a jacket and a phone.

---

## How to reach the trailhead by transit

[TransLink Route 236](https://www.translink.ca/schedules-and-maps/route/236/direction/0/schedule) runs from Lonsdale Quay to the Grouse Mountain Skyride terminus at stop **54290**. Riders should set the travel date in TransLink’s schedule or Trip Planner because departure times vary by day.

Driving does not remove the need to check access first. Metro Vancouver lists pay parking at Lot D, but a parking space does not mean the Grouse Grind is open.

The practical rule is to verify three things on the day of the trip: the trail status, the mountain’s download operations and the Route 236 schedule or parking plan. If any part is unavailable, choose a different outing rather than improvising on the mountain.
