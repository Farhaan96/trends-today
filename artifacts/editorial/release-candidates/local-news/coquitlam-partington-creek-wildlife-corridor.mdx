Coquitlam is moving a Burke Mountain wildlife-corridor plan through its development process, with the city saying the protected area would follow Partington Creek and connect major green spaces around the northeast neighbourhood.

## The proposed corridor would protect a creek-side link between parks

The City of Coquitlam says the corridor would preserve a wildlife connection between **Pinecone Burke Provincial Park**, **Minnekhada Regional Park**, and surrounding Burke Mountain green spaces.

The protected area includes a salmon-bearing creek. The city says the first phase would be secured between **Cedar Drive and Victoria Drive** as part of a proposed townhouse development in the area.

Key corridor details:

- **50-metre corridor**: Planned along Partington Creek.
- **100-metre buffer**: Proposed at a wildlife hot spot identified by monitoring.
- **Parkland transfer**: Lands around Partington Creek would move to city ownership and be designated as parkland if the proposal proceeds.

## Monitoring found heavy wildlife use on Burke Mountain

The city says a **15-month wildlife monitoring study** was commissioned before development could occur in the area.

According to the city, motion-activated cameras captured more than **400,000 wildlife images** and documented **57 species**, including species at risk. The study also recorded more than **31,000 images of black bears** across more than **2,600 independent events**.

The city says the study documented cougars, bobcats, river otters, coyotes, bald eagles, barred owls, western spotted skunks, beavers, and great blue herons.

## The approval process is tied to townhouse projects

The first phase is being secured through three related Burke Mountain development projects. Coquitlam says those projects include more than **400 townhouse units**, transportation improvements, and additional parkland dedication.

The city says the first development proposal within the corridor received first reading from council on **May 25, 2026**. After a **June 22, 2026** public hearing, council granted second and third readings.

What residents should watch:

- **Final approvals**: The corridor protection depends on the related development moving forward.
- **Protected share**: If the development proceeds, about **30%** of the identified wildlife corridor would be protected.
- **Local park changes**: The proposal would expand Knoll Park and create a larger natural buffer beside Partington Creek.
